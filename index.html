Published Journal Article (2026) Canadian Geotechnical Journal

Mitigation of CPT thrust force through glauconite sand

Zachary J. Westgate, Asitha Senanayake, Morteza Shafiei, and Don J. DeGroot

Investigated methods to increase piezocone penetration (CPTu) depth in authigenic glauconite sand deposits (Hornerstown and Navesink formations) as part of the Piling in Glauconitic Sand (PIGS) Joint Industry Project at the New Jersey Search Farm test site. Demonstrates that while standard friction reducers lower thrust by 50%6–80%, enlarged diameter friction reducers (48 mm) and upper-bevel water jetting (4 × 3 mm ports at 6-7 L/min) effectively mitigate rod friction and negative pore pressure buildup, extending penetration depth from ~14 m to 23.5 m.

DOI: 10.1139/cgj-2026-0117

Published Journal Article (Dec 2023 / 2024) Granular Matter (Springer) · Vol. 26, Art. 10

Quantifying three-dimensional sphericity indices of irregular fine particles from 2D images through sequential sieving tests

Farzad Kaviani-Hamedani, Mohammad Esmailzade, Kianoush Adineh, Morteza Shafiei, and Danial Shirkavand

Formulated a multi-time-step sequential sieving test method and developed empirical predictive equations relating 2D optical projection circularities to true 3D particle sphericity indices and Zingg flatness/elongation aspect ratios. Benchmarked and validated against 3D X-ray Micro-CT scan tomography across irregular sand grains and 7 synthetic shapes, showing that 3D image-processing grading curves align precisely with mechanical sieve analysis.

Springer DOI: 10.1007/s10035-023-01380-z

Published Journal Article (2023) Canadian Geotechnical Journal ·- Vol. 60, No. 4, pp. 488–503

Investigation of soil setup effects on pile response in clay considering overconsolidation ratio and installation method through physical modeling

Kazem Fakharian, Morteza Shafiei, and Salar Hafezan

Constructed a pressurized calibration chamber and custom instrumented CPT-scale model pile equipped with 3 miniature piezometers and 3 strain gauges. Executed 21 static load tests (SLTs) in Kaolinite clay at OCR 1.2 (NC) and OCR 4.8 (OC) from 1 hour to 90 days. Separated the contribution of excess pore water pressure (EPWP) dissipation (<3 days: +287% driven NC friction increase) from secondary soil aging (>3 days: +28.8% driven NC increase) and formulated a quantitative pile stiffness evolution relation.

DOI: 10.1139/cgj-2021-0686

Teaching Philosophy & Case Study Engineering Ethics

The "2 A.M. Caliper Rule": Why Dimensional Precision Safeguards Human Lives

In geotechnical and structural education, I instill in my students that calculations are not abstract academic exercises—they govern physical boundaries.

Case Audit: Space Shuttle Challenger (31°F / -0.5°C)

At 31°F, the primary synthetic rubber O-ring lost resiliency, exhibiting a thermal gap contraction of 0.054 mm compared to its standard 0.040 mm extrusion tolerance. Had the engineering team performed a physical caliper audit rather than relying on qualitative assurances, the launch refusal would have been unambiguous.

Whether auditing pile driveability in calibration chambers or verifying CPTu thrust limits in authigenic glauconite sand, rigorous physical auditing and strict empirical scrutiny remain the bedrock of geotechnical reliability.
